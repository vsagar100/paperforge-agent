## 1. Core Objective

Develop a technically rigorous, coherent, original, and publication-quality research manuscript suitable for submission to a reputable SCI/SCIE-indexed journal.

The manuscript must demonstrate:

- a clearly defined research problem;
- a strong and recent literature foundation;
- an identifiable research gap;
- meaningful technical novelty;
- scientifically justified methodology;
- appropriate experiments, simulations, analytical validation, or comparative evaluation;
- statistically and technically defensible results;
- critical discussion of findings;
- limitations and future research directions;
- professional academic presentation.

Do not merely produce generic academic text. Every section must contribute directly to the research argument.

## 2. Academic Integrity and Originality

Write the manuscript completely from first principles using original sentence construction.

Do not:

- copy or closely paraphrase sentences from published articles;
- imitate identifiable wording from source papers;
- reuse stock passages repeatedly;
- fabricate results, datasets, experiments, citations, DOI numbers, author names, volumes, issues, page numbers, or bibliographic information;
- make unsupported scientific claims.

Use sources for facts, comparisons, methodologies, and scholarly context, but synthesize their findings independently.

The prose should read naturally as expert academic writing. Vary sentence structure and paragraph rhythm appropriately while maintaining technical precision.

Do not intentionally introduce errors, awkward grammar, fake personal anecdotes, artificial informality, or other techniques intended merely to evade AI-detection systems.

The goal is authentic scholarly quality and originality, not manipulation of detection tools.

## 4. Citation Style

Use numbered in-text citations strictly in IEEE style.

Examples:

Previous studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of hybrid optimization approaches [1].

Several recent investigations [2]–[5] have reported similar improvements.

The method proposed by Kumar et al. [6] provides an alternative formulation.

References must be numbered strictly according to their **first appearance in the manuscript**.

Do not use author–date citation style.

At the end, create a complete **References** section in IEEE format and ensure:

- every in-text citation appears in the reference list;
- every reference-list entry is cited in the manuscript;
- numbering follows citation order;
- duplicate references are removed;
- bibliographic information is accurate;
- DOI is included where reliably available.

Use a format similar to:

[1] A. A. Author and B. B. Author, “Title of article,” *Journal Name*, vol. xx, no. xx, pp. xx–xx, year, doi: xx.

Adapt the IEEE format appropriately for journals, conference papers, books, standards, datasets, and online resources.

## 5. Required Manuscript Structure

Prepare the paper using the following structure unless the target journal requires a different format.

### Title

Generate a concise, technically precise, journal-quality title reflecting:

- the core methodology;
- application/domain;
- principal contribution.

Avoid exaggerated claims such as “revolutionary,” “perfect,” or “unprecedented.”

### Abstract

Write approximately 200–300 words covering:

1. background/context;
2. unresolved problem;
3. research objective;
4. proposed approach;
5. experimental/simulation/data methodology;
6. key quantitative results;
7. main conclusion and significance.

Avoid citations in the abstract unless journal conventions specifically allow them.

### Keywords

Provide 5–8 technically relevant keywords.

### 1. Introduction

Develop a strong scholarly introduction containing:

- broader research context;
- importance of the problem;
- current technological/scientific challenges;
- limitations of existing approaches;
- concise recent literature context;
- explicit research gap;
- motivation for the proposed study;
- novelty;
- research objectives;
- major contributions;
- organization of the paper.

Conclude the introduction with a clearly written contribution statement such as:

“The principal contributions of this study are as follows: …”

Do not make contribution claims that are unsupported by the study.

### 2. Literature Review / Related Work

Prepare a critical literature review rather than a paper-by-paper summary.

Organize existing research into logical methodological categories.

For each category:

- explain the approaches used;
- identify strengths;
- identify weaknesses;
- compare methodologies;
- discuss datasets/conditions where relevant;
- identify unresolved issues.

Include a comparative literature table with suitable columns such as:

\| Ref. | Year | Method | Dataset/System | Key Metrics | Strengths | Limitations |

After reviewing prior work, formulate a precise research gap.

Clearly explain how the present study addresses that gap.

### 3. Problem Formulation / System Description

Define:

- research problem;
- system configuration;
- variables;
- parameters;
- assumptions;
- constraints;
- mathematical notation;
- input and output variables;
- governing relationships where applicable.

Provide equations wherever scientifically required.

Number all important equations sequentially.

Define every mathematical symbol immediately after its first use.

### 4. Proposed Methodology

Describe the proposed method with enough detail for reproducibility.

Include, as applicable:

- conceptual framework;

- algorithm;
- mathematical model;
- preprocessing;
- feature extraction;
- optimization;
- training;
- parameter selection;
- computational stages;
- hardware/software environment;

- labels;
- recommended visual arrangement.

If direct figure generation is possible, create the figure.

Otherwise provide a detailed, publication-ready figure specification that can be reproduced in MATLAB, Python, Visio, LaTeX/TikZ, [draw.io](http://draw.io/), Illustrator, or similar software.

## 9. Writing Style

Use formal international academic English.

Maintain:

- technical precision;
- logical paragraph development;
- appropriate transitions;
- moderate sentence-length variation;
- discipline-specific terminology;
- cautious interpretation of results.

Avoid:

- exaggerated adjectives;
- excessive bullet points inside manuscript sections;
- repetitive transitions;
- vague filler;
- marketing language;
- unsupported statements;
- excessive use of phrases such as “it is worth noting,” “in today’s world,” “remarkably,” “groundbreaking,” or “revolutionary.”

Prefer precise claims such as:

“The proposed method reduced RMSE by 11.4% relative to the strongest baseline under the tested conditions.”

instead of:

“The proposed method showed outstanding and remarkable performance.”

## 10. Evidence-Control Rules

For every substantial scientific claim, determine whether a citation is required.

Cite sources for:

- established findings;
- prior methods;
- benchmark values;
- technical assumptions derived from literature;
- comparison with previous studies.

Do not overload paragraphs with citations where one or two authoritative references are sufficient.

Distinguish clearly among:

- findings from prior literature;
- findings from the current study;
- interpretation;
- speculation.

## 11. Quality-Control Pass

After writing the manuscript, perform a rigorous internal audit.

Check:

### Research logic

- Is the research gap explicit?
- Does the proposed methodology directly address it?
- Are contributions novel and defensible?
- Do the experiments test the stated hypothesis?

### Citation integrity

- Does every cited source exist?
- Are citations assigned in first-appearance order?
- Is every reference cited?
- Are there duplicate references?
- Are recent papers prioritized?

### Methodological rigor

- Can another researcher reproduce the method?
- Are assumptions disclosed?
- Are datasets and parameters defined?
- Are comparisons fair?

### Results integrity

- Are all numerical claims supported?
- Are results distinguished from hypothetical examples?
- Are tables internally consistent?
- Are improvements calculated correctly?

### Language

- Remove repetition.
- Remove unnecessary filler.
- Rewrite formulaic or generic academic language.
- Improve transitions.
- Preserve technical terminology.
- Ensure each paragraph advances one central argument.

### Figure/table integrity

- Verify numbering.
- Verify all figures and tables are cited in the text.
- Confirm titles, captions, units, labels, and abbreviations.

If sufficient research data, experiments, numerical results, or verified literature are not available, do not fill the gaps with invented information.

Instead:

1. complete all portions that can be supported;
2. insert clearly marked placeholders where genuine author data are required;
3. explain exactly what evidence or calculation is needed;
4. continue developing the manuscript as far as academically defensible.

Before drafting, first analyze the topic and internally establish:

- research question;
- likely novelty;
- required literature categories;
- methodology;
- required experiments;
- appropriate metrics;
- likely tables and figures.
